# Code of Conduct

GT-STACER is a small, multilingual community building free software. The
rules below keep it that way.

## What we expect

- **Be respectful.** Critique code, not people. Assume good faith.
- **Be specific.** Vague complaints help no one. If something is wrong,
  point at the line, the file, the commit.
- **Be patient.** Many contributors are not native English speakers, and
  most of us are part-time. Slow responses aren't disrespect.
- **Stay on topic.** Project channels exist to improve the software, not to
  debate unrelated politics, religion, or national/ethnic questions.

## What's unacceptable

- Personal attacks, slurs, sustained harassment, or threats — public or
  private.
- Doxxing or publishing anyone's personal info without their consent.
- Posting sexually explicit material or violent imagery in any project
  channel.
- Persisting in unwelcome behaviour after being asked to stop.

The above applies to **all project spaces**: GitHub issues and PRs,
commit messages, code comments, the project's website discussion sections,
and any chat room flying the GT-STACER name.

## Reporting

If someone is making the project unsafe or unwelcoming, contact the
maintainer:

- Email **`gnutux.arabic@gmail.com`** with subject prefix
  `[GT-STACER conduct]`.
- Or use GitHub's private "Report abuse" link on the offending content.

Reports stay confidential. We won't reveal your identity to the reported
party without your explicit permission.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides. Typical outcomes, escalating:

1. **Private note** — a quiet message asking the behaviour to stop.
2. **Public warning** — a comment on the thread explaining what's
   unacceptable about it.
3. **Temporary ban** — a fixed-duration block from the relevant channel
   (issues, PRs, etc).
4. **Permanent ban** — for severe or repeated violations.

Bans cover all GT-STACER spaces, not just the channel the incident
happened in.

## Why this exists

This document is loosely based on the
[Contributor Covenant 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/),
stripped down to what we actually intend to enforce. If you want the
full text, it's at the link above. The point isn't bureaucracy — it's so
contributors know what to expect and what's expected of them, and so they
know who to talk to when something goes wrong.
